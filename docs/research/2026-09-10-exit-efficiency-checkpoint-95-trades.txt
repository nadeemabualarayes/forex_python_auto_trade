A. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

  Question asked: are the trades that moved favourably before hitting SL a recurring exit-management
  problem, or normal noise?

  Answer from 95 post-fix trades: NOT primarily an exit problem, and no implementation bug was found.

  1. Losers rarely got far. The 39 losing trades have a median MFE of 0.20R and a mean of 0.26R.
     NONE of them ever reached +1.0R. Only 8 of 39 (20.5%) reached +0.5R and only 3 (7.7%) reached
     +0.75R. The impression of "it was in profit and gave it all back" comes from 17 trades that
     reached at least +0.25R, and their best excursion averaged +0.51R with a maximum of +0.99R.

  2. The decisive counterfactual runs the other way. Any stop or breakeven rule tight enough to
     rescue the 8 losers that reached +0.5R would also have breached the 17 winners (30.4% of all
     winners) whose MAE was >= 0.5R. The current structure is roughly balanced, not leaking.

  3. Where capture genuinely is low is the trailing stop: 33 managed exits averaged +0.76R of MFE and
     realised +0.19R, a median efficiency of 0.24. But every one of those 33 is a WINNER. The trailing
     stop is converting trades into small profits, not destroying large ones, so "low efficiency"
     there is the mechanism working as designed rather than a defect.

  4. The dominant problem is entry quality in the 07-12 session, not exits. Morning trades go against
     the entry immediately and far: median MAE 1.08R versus 0.50R in the other sessions, and 59.5% of
     them reach a full -1R adverse excursion versus 25.9% elsewhere. Their median MFE is 0.53R against
     0.74R elsewhere. 62% of morning trades end at a full stop versus 28% elsewhere. The morning is
     entering positions that were wrong from the first minute; better exits could not have saved them.

  5. Sizing and execution are clean. All 95 trades were terminal-priced. The lot correctly scales with
     the stop (0.01 x86, 0.02 x9 - narrow stops let $5 buy two minimum lots). Five trades (5.3%)
     realised more than $5 of risk, all 0.01-lot trades where the market fill came in wider than the
     quoted stop; the worst overshoot was $0.99. No sizing fault.

  6. Correction to an earlier verbal report: lots are NOT always 0.01. Nine post-fix trades were sized
     at 0.02 because their stop was narrow enough for the budget to afford it. That is correct
     behaviour and it was mis-stated in an earlier chat summary.

  sensitivity: dropping the partial entry/exit bars lowers MFE R by 0.168 on average (78 trades have a fully-interior bar)
  method:
    - lifetime = broker entry fill -> broker exit fill; excursions from M1 bars whose minute falls in
      [entry minute, exit minute]. The first and last bars are partial, so the headline MFE/MAE is an
      UPPER bound; the sensitivity line above shows the effect of dropping them.
    - no intrabar path is assumed: only each bar's high and low are used, never their order.
    - bars are BID. Longs mark at the bid (bars used as-is); shorts mark at the ask, so each bar's own
      stored spread is added to its high and low before measuring a short.
    - R = |entry fill - initial SL| from the ENTRY journal row. Realised R = broker net / (R x 100 x lot).
    - exit efficiency = realised R / MFE R, computed only where MFE R > 0.
    - 'SL' = stop hit with no stop move; 'TRAIL' = stop hit after a breakeven/trail move; 'TP' = target.

L. WHAT THE DATA SUGGESTS
==================================================================================================

  L1. There is no exit-management bug.
      Full stops realise exactly -1.000R (mean, min and max all -1.000), targets realise +0.93R on
      average, and not one trade shows a realised R above its measured MFE. The machinery does what
      it says. The "gave back a winner" cases exist but are small and few: 17 trades, mean +0.51R of
      forgone excursion, 8.6R in total across the whole 95-trade set.

  L2. The 07-12 session is an ENTRY problem, and it is the whole loss.
      07-12: 37 trades, 37.8% win, -$57.19. Everything else together: 58 trades, 72.4% win, +$21.22.
      The morning's signature is adverse-from-the-start, not badly-exited:
        - median MAE 1.08R vs 0.50R elsewhere; 59.5% hit a full -1R adverse move vs 25.9% elsewhere
        - median MFE 0.53R vs 0.74R elsewhere
        - exit mix 23 SL / 7 TRAIL / 7 TP vs 16 SL / 26 TRAIL / 16 TP elsewhere
        - both directions are equally bad (LONG -0.43R, SHORT -0.38R), so it is not a directional bias
        - worst in the LOW ATR tercile (-0.63R avg) and in medium/deep penetration
        - it lost on all three days it traded: -19.89, -8.34, -28.96
      A mean-reversion rule with no trend filter is fading the London-open expansion; that is the
      most plausible mechanism, but this data cannot confirm the mechanism.

  L3. Penetration depth orders exactly as the excursion data would predict.
      small: MFE 0.81R / MAE 0.56R, +$28.40, 77.4% win
      touch: MFE 0.71R / MAE 0.67R, -$2.01
      deep:  MFE 0.58R / MAE 0.87R, -$27.41
      medium:MFE 0.50R / MAE 0.84R, -$34.95
      Shallow entries get more favourable movement and less adverse movement than deep ones. The
      medium bucket being worse than deep is the one inversion and looks like small-sample noise.

  L4. 17-24 LONG does look structurally different, on 12 trades.
      MFE 0.84R avg vs 0.59R for other longs; MAE 0.54R vs 0.84R. The excursion profile, not just the
      P&L, separates it. This directly contradicts backtest Experiment 3, which predicted evening
      longs were structurally WEAK. Twelve trades cannot settle it either way, but it is now clear
      that adopting the Experiment 3 filter would have been a mistake.

  L5. The 2.0/2.0 ATR structure - evidence both ways, no conclusion drawn.
      Against the current structure:
        - only 23.2% of trades ever reach +1.0R MFE, so the target is unreachable for three trades in four
        - 66.3% reach +0.5R, so a nearer target would convert far more trades
        - median MFE (0.72R) and median MAE (0.72R) are identical: the typical trade travels about as
          far against as it does for, which is not what a 1:1 target/stop wants to see
      For the current structure:
        - the 23 TP exits produce $85.43 of the $108.99 of gross profit; a nearer target halves them
        - a 0.5R stop would have breached 30.4% of the winners while saving only 20.5% of the losers
        - the trailing stop already harvests the sub-1R moves: 33 managed exits, all winners
      Net: the structure is not obviously mismatched, and the two adjustments that look attractive in
      isolation (nearer target, tighter stop) each cost more than they gain on this sample.

==================================================================================================
M. WHAT THE DATA DOES NOT PROVE
==================================================================================================

  M1. Sample size. 95 trades over four days, from a single instrument, in one volatility regime.
      Sub-groups are far smaller: 17-24 LONG is 12 trades, HIGH ATR is 13, the 07-12 LOW-ATR cell is 12.
      Nothing here reaches statistical significance and no cell should be acted on alone.

  M2. Four days is not a regime sample. The whole window is 7-10 September 2026. A single news cycle,
      a single directional bias in gold, or one unusual London session could produce the entire 07-12
      result. Three losing mornings in a row is suggestive, not conclusive.

  M3. Intrabar path is unknown. MFE and MAE come from M1 highs and lows. Whether price reached the
      favourable extreme before or after the adverse one inside a bar cannot be recovered, so any
      "we could have exited at +X" reading is an upper bound, not an achievable result.

  M4. Partial first and last bars inflate the headline excursions. Dropping them lowers MFE by 0.168R
      on average. The direction of every comparison in this report survives that adjustment, but the
      absolute levels are optimistic.

  M5. Counterfactual stop/target levels are bounds, not simulations. "A 0.5R stop would have breached
      30.4% of winners" assumes the adverse extreme was touched, which the bar data supports, but it
      does not model what the trailing logic would then have done differently, nor the different
      sequence of trades that would have followed (one position at a time means every changed exit
      changes which later signals are even seen).

  M6. Selection pressure. The session, penetration and ATR splits were all chosen after looking at
      this same data. That is the exact error that made backtest Experiments 1-3 inconclusive, and
      L4 shows one of those experiments has now been contradicted by forward data.

  M7. This profile is not the evaluation profile. M1, RSI 35/65, no trend filter, no candle
      confirmation, trailing on. Nothing here transfers to the evaluation bot on main.

  M8. Manual interference exists in the account. Four positions opened by the strategy were closed by
      hand from the mobile terminal, and six unrelated manual XAUUSD orders plus two GBPUSD orders
      were placed on 8-9 September. Those are excluded from every number above, but they show the
      account is not a sealed experiment.
